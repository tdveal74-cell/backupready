Generator Maintenance Schedule: How to Keep a Backup Generator Ready

Updated October 2026 · 8 min read · Independent maintenance guide

A generator is only backup power if it actually starts when the grid goes down. The single biggest reason generators fail during an outage isn't age or a bad unit — it's months of sitting untouched with stale fuel, a dead battery, or oil that was never changed. The maintenance itself is simple and cheap; the failures that come from skipping it are not. Here's the schedule.

Weekly and biweekly tasks

  • Exercise the engine. Standby units with an auto-exercise feature handle this on their own — confirm it's enabled. Portable generators should run under a small load for 10-15 minutes every 1-2 weeks, even with no outage.
  • Check the control panel for fault codes or warning lights, right on the panel or companion app.
  • Listen for anything off — rough idle, excessive smoke, or new noises during the exercise cycle.

Monthly tasks

  • Check oil level and condition. Dark, gritty, or watery oil means an early change is due.
  • Inspect the air filter and clean or replace as needed.
  • Check battery terminals (standby units) for corrosion and confirm the trickle charger works — a dead battery is the most common no-start cause.
  • Inspect fuel lines for cracking, leaks, or loose clamps.
  • Clear vents and intakes of leaves, snow, or vegetation around standby units.

Annual / seasonal tasks

  • Full oil and filter change. Portable: every 50-100 hours or once a season. Standby: every 100-200 hours or annually.
  • Replace air filter and spark plug on portable units — cheap parts that meaningfully affect reliability.
  • Stabilize or rotate fuel. Gasoline degrades in as little as 30 days. Propane/natural gas standby units avoid this problem entirely.
  • Test the transfer switch or interlock (see our hookup options guide) to confirm it switches cleanly both ways.
  • Schedule a professional service visit for standby units — required by most manufacturers to keep the warranty valid.

Warning signs it needs service now

  • Hard starting — often a fuel, spark plug, or battery issue.
  • Stalling under load — a dirty carburetor, bad fuel, or undersized unit.
  • Visible oil or fuel leaks — stop using the unit until inspected.
  • Error codes on a standby panel — often a low-battery or low-coolant condition that will cause a failed auto-start.
  • Excessive vibration or new noise — a loose mount or failing part worth catching early.

FAQ

How often should a standby generator run a self-test?
Most are factory-set to self-exercise weekly for 12-20 minutes unloaded. If not enabled, run it manually every 1-2 weeks.
How often does generator oil need to be changed?
Portable: after the first 20-30 hours, then every 50-100 hours or once a season. Standby: every 100-200 hours or annually.
What happens if a generator sits unused for months?
Gasoline degrades in as little as 30 days, batteries self-discharge, and oil loses lubricity — often causing a no-start when it's needed. Weekly exercise and fuel stabilizer prevent most of this.
