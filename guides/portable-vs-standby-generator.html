Portable vs. Standby Generators: Which One Actually Fits Your Situation

Updated October 2026 · 8 min read · Independent buying guide

The honest answer depends on three things: how much you can spend upfront, how much hands-on effort you're willing to do during an outage, and how much of the house you actually need to keep running.

Side-by-side comparison

FactorPortableStandby
Typical cost$500–$2,000 unit; $4,000–$7,000 installed$9,000–$23,000+ installed

When a portable makes sense

  • Budget is the deciding factor — often 1/4 to 1/3 the price of standby.
  • Outages are infrequent and short where you live.
  • You're usually home when outages happen.
  • You rent, or aren't ready to commit to a permanent installation.

When a standby makes sense

  • You travel or aren't reliably home.
  • Someone depends on powered medical equipment.
  • You want the whole house covered, including central AC.
  • Outages in your area are frequent, long, or seasonal.

How much power do you need?

Refrigerator ~600–800W, sump pump ~800–1,500W, window AC ~1,000–1,500W, well pump ~1,000–2,000W, central AC ~3,000–5,000W (often rules out smaller portables). See our sizing guide to calculate your exact load, or the cost guide for installed pricing.

Safety: the part people skip

Portable generators kill people every year from carbon monoxide poisoning — never run one in a garage or near windows/doors. Never plug a generator directly into a wall outlet (backfeeding) — it can electrocute utility workers and is illegal in most places. See our safe hookup guide.
