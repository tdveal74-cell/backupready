Portable Generator Buying Guide: What Actually Matters

Updated October 2026 · 8 min read · Independent buying guide

Once you know roughly what size you need (see our sizing guide), the next decision is which features are worth paying for. Prices for similar wattage can range from a few hundred dollars to well over a thousand — here's what drives that gap.

Inverter vs. conventional

Conventional generators use a simpler engine-driven alternator — cheaper per watt, rugged, fine for lights, heaters, basic pumps, but "dirtier" power. Inverter generators produce a clean, stable sine wave similar to grid power — cost more but are quieter and safe for laptops, phones, and modern appliances with circuit boards.

Rule of thumb: if your list includes anything with a circuit board you can't afford to damage, pay for the inverter.

Fuel type: gas, propane, or dual-fuel

FuelProsCons
GasolineCheapest, most availableDegrades in storage (3-6 months)

Run time and noise

Manufacturers list run time at 25% or 50% load, not full load — check which before comparing numbers. For overnight use, look for 8+ hours at 50% load. Conventional units run 70-80 dB at 23 ft; inverter models are usually 50-65 dB. Check local noise ordinances before buying.

Features worth paying for

  • Electric start — worth it for bad-weather 2am starts.
  • CO shutoff sensor — auto-shuts down if carbon monoxide builds up. A safety feature, not a luxury.
  • Parallel capability — run two smaller units together when you need more power.
  • Extended warranty — check what's covered; many only cover the engine, not the alternator.

Pre-purchase checklist

  • Confirm running + starting watts cover your sizing worksheet.
  • Decide inverter vs. conventional based on what you're powering.
  • Check the dB rating against your neighborhood.
  • Confirm it has a CO shutoff sensor.
  • Decide how you'll connect it — see our transfer switch vs. interlock kit guide.
