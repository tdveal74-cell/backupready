Whole-House Generator Cost in 2026: What You'll Actually Pay

Updated October 2026 · 9 min read · Independent buying guide

If you're pricing a whole-house standby generator, the honest answer is: it depends almost entirely on the size (kW) you need and how much work it takes to connect it. Below are realistic 2026 installed-price ranges by size.

Cost by generator size

These are installed ranges — the generator plus an automatic transfer switch, gas/electrical hookup, pad, and permit. Always get a written, itemized quote from a licensed local installer.

Generator sizeInstalled price rangeTypically covers
Portable (manual switch)$4,000 – $7,000A handful of circuits; you connect it yourself
8 kW standby$9,000 – $17,000Essentials: heat, fridge, well pump, sump pump
14 kW standby (most common)$11,000 – $19,500Most of a small-to-mid home, often one central AC
22 kW standby$13,000 – $23,000A larger home including central AC
Liquid-cooled 25 kW+$21,000 – $37,000Large homes, long run-times
Why ranges vary so much: two homes needing the same size unit can land $5,000+ apart depending on gas line run length, whether you need a propane tank, and local permit fees.

What drives the price

Biggest swing factors: gas line run length, propane tank needs, electrical run distance, and permit fees.

What size do you need?

8-10kW covers essentials. 14kW is the most common size, covering most homes including one central AC. 22-24kW covers a larger home. A licensed electrician should size it from your panel.
