How to Size a Backup Generator for Your Home (Without Guessing)

Updated October 2026 · 9 min read · Independent buying guide

Sizing is where most people overspend on a unit they don't need, or undersize and trip the generator the first time two appliances kick on at once. Here's the method electricians use, simplified.

Running watts vs. starting watts

Running watts are what an appliance draws once it's on. Starting watts are the short burst of extra power a motor needs for a second when it first switches on — often 2–3x the running wattage. Size for your running total plus the single largest starting surge, not the sum of all starting watts.

The load worksheet

Add up all running watts for what you want backed up. Then find the single largest starting-watt surge on your list and add that on top, plus a 10–20% safety margin.

Typical sizing by home type

  • Essentials only (fridge, sump pump, lights): 3,000–5,000W portable.
  • Essentials + window AC or well pump: 5,000–7,500W portable, or 10–12kW standby.
  • Small-to-mid home without central AC: 10–14kW standby.
  • Mid-to-large home with central AC: 16–22kW standby.
  • Large/all-electric home: 22–26kW+, sized by an electrician.

Sizing mistakes to avoid

  • Sizing off square footage instead of actual loads.
  • Ignoring starting watts entirely — a unit can stall when a compressor kicks on.
  • Forgetting seasonal loads — size for whichever season's combined load is higher.
  • Skipping a professional load calculation above portable size.
